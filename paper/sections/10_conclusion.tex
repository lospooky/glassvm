\section{Conclusion}

GlassVM separates machine semantics from experimental orchestration and
makes observation a negotiated, caller-owned contract. Typed synchronous
emissions, streaming normalizers, and separate evidence and recorder
accounting provide a common execution path while leaving machine-specific
behavior with each bundle. The reference recorder supplies one durable
publication path without requiring downstream consumers to read it or
construct a canonical whole-run trace.

The three-machine implementation and the planned sealed evaluation provide
a concrete setting for assessing this design. Final claims about memory,
observation cost, storage, Python sink overhead, and semantic equivalence
remain unresolved until their sealed receipts are incorporated. The results
will determine the empirical scope of the paper; the interface design alone
does not establish universal performance, constant memory, or
substrate-independent computation.
